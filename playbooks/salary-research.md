# Salary research

Use this when the user asks "what should I ask for?", when a form needs a number, or when a recruiter asks for expectations. Put the result in the report's Salary section, with sources.

## Steps

1. **Use the posting first.** A published range (Ashby and some Greenhouse postings include one) beats any estimate.
2. **Search 2–4 sources** for the same title, seniority and city: salary aggregators for that country, recruiter salary guides, and similar postings that publish a range. Note the date and sample size, and treat tiny samples as weak.
3. **Adjust for the specifics:**
   - **Seniority and scope:** lead roles, team size.
   - **Scarce skills:** AI evaluation, performance engineering, security.
   - **Domain:** banking and payments usually pay more.
   - **Contract vs permanent:** contracts pay more to make up for no benefits and no job security.
4. **Give a range and a floor:** "Ask for X–Y; don't go below Z", with one or two sentences of reasoning. Say clearly that it's a judgment call.

## Contracts and day rates

- **Turning a monthly figure into a day rate:** about 21–22 working days a month, minus unpaid leave and public holidays. For example, monthly × 12 ÷ (number of working days you'll actually be paid for).
- **Ask what's included:** housing, transport, medical insurance, annual flights, visa costs, end-of-service benefits. "All-inclusive" rates need to be higher.
- **Employer of record (EOR):** the agency employs you and the client directs the work. Check who issues the visa, how payroll and leave work, and what happens when the contract ends.

## Common traps

- **Mixing currencies or periods:** annual vs monthly, gross vs net, tax-free vs taxed.
- **Comparing across countries** without allowing for the cost of living.
- **Anchoring too low:** if the user's current salary is low for the market, base the ask on the market, not on their current pay.
- **Quoting a single number on a form** when a range would do. Use "open to discussing based on the full package" if the user prefers.
