# Visa and work-rights checks

Work rights are the most common reason a good application goes nowhere. Check them for **every** job outside the user's current country, and put the result in the report's "Check before applying" section.

## Steps

1. **Know the user's status.** It should be in the master resume or experience bank: citizenship, current visa, which countries they can work in, and whether they need sponsorship. If it's missing, ask once and record it with `npm run add`.
2. **Read the posting:** "must have the right to work in...", "no sponsorship", "visa support available", "relocation package". Those statements override everything else.
3. **If the posting is silent,** check the official sponsor register or the scheme below for that country, and search for "<company> visa sponsorship <role>" (one search).
4. **Report what you found and how sure you are,** with a link. Never say "they sponsor" without evidence. Say "I found no evidence either way" when that's the truth.
5. **Suggest the action:** ask the recruiter before investing time, apply anyway, or skip.

## Country notes

Thresholds and rules change, often every year. Always confirm current figures on the official site before quoting a number.

| Country | Where to check | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **United Kingdom** | GOV.UK "Register of licensed sponsors: workers" (a downloadable list) | The employer must hold a sponsor licence. Skilled Worker visas have salary and occupation-code thresholds |
| **Netherlands** | IND "Public register of recognised sponsors" | Highly skilled migrant visas need a recognised sponsor and a minimum salary that depends on age (updated each January). Many Dutch postings also require Dutch |
| **Ireland** | Department of Enterprise employment permits pages | The Critical Skills Employment Permit covers many tech roles above a salary threshold; the General Employment Permit is an alternative. Any employer can apply |
| **Germany** | Make it in Germany / Federal Foreign Office | The EU Blue Card has a yearly salary threshold (lower for shortage occupations, which include IT) and needs a recognised degree. German may be required by the employer |
| **Australia** | Department of Home Affairs (Skills in Demand / subclass 482, employer nomination) | The employer must sponsor; many postings say "Australian work rights required", which means no sponsorship |
| **United States** | USCIS (H-1B) | H-1B is a lottery with fixed annual timing, so few employers sponsor new hires from abroad. Treat "sponsorship not stated" as unlikely |
| **Canada** | IRCC | Usually needs an LMIA-backed offer or permanent residence (Express Entry). Postings rarely sponsor |
| **UAE / GCC** | — | The employer normally arranges the work visa. Note whether the role expects a local hire (already in the country) or offers relocation, and whether a contract is via an agency or employer of record |

## How to phrase it in the report

> **UK sponsorship:** you need it, and the posting doesn't mention it. Example Systems Ltd doesn't appear on the GOV.UK register of licensed sponsors under that name (checked {date}, [link]). Ask the recruiter before investing time.

> **Local role:** you're already in Abu Dhabi/Dubai, so there's no visa issue. Ask who sponsors the visa on a contract (agency or client).
